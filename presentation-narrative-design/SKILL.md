# Presentation: Narrative Design

## Purpose
Structure complex technical information into a compelling, logical story for slide decks, demos, or technical talks.

## When to Use
- When preparing for a product demo or investor pitch.
- When creating a "Post-Mortem" or technical retrospective.
- When explaining a new vision or strategy to a team.

## When NOT to Use
- For raw data dumps or internal spreadsheets.
- For one-on-one technical deep dives (use Research Deep Dive instead).

## Inputs
- Technical facts, data points, or project outcomes.
- Target audience (Execs vs. Engineers).

## Outputs
- Presentation structure:
  - The "Hook" (Why should they care?).
  - Problem/Opportunity statement.
  - Solution/Innovation walkthrough.
  - Proof/Data (The "So What?").
  - Call to action (The "What's Next?").

## Rules
- **One Idea per Slide**: Avoid overwhelming the audience with text.
- **Show, Don't Just Tell**: Use visuals or live demos for the "Wow" factor.
- **Audience Calibration**: Adjust technical depth based on the room.

## Failure Modes
- Reading from the slides instead of telling a story.
- Buried the lede (Waiting too long to explain the value).

## Checklist
- [ ] Identify the "Single Most Important Message".
- [ ] Map the emotional arc (Tension -> Resolution).
- [ ] Create a "Zero-Slide" version (elevator pitch).
- [ ] Verify that visuals support the verbal narrative.
- [ ] Practice the transitions between key points.
