# Research Synthesis Frameworks

## Purpose
Connect disparate concepts across multiple research papers or data sources to form a coherent, unified understanding or a new thesis.

## When to Use
- When building a "state of the art" overview for a domain.
- When identifying trends across multiple product launches or research releases.
- When needing to bridge the gap between theoretical research and practical implementation.

## When NOT to Use
- For summarizing a single source (use Research Paper Distillation instead).
- For simple fact-checking.

## Inputs
- Multiple distilled summaries or raw research inputs.
- A central theme or synthesis goal.

## Outputs
- A synthesis report featuring:
  - Convergence analysis (Where do sources agree?).
  - Contradiction analysis (Where do they differ?).
  - Capability mapping (What can we do now that we couldn't before?).
  - Strategic recommendations.

## Rules
- Cite sources for every synthesis point.
- Group insights by capability or theme, not by source.
- Highlight "delta" - what has changed since the last major synthesis.

## Failure Modes
- "Stacking" summaries instead of synthesizing (listing papers one by one).
- Over-generalizing and losing specific nuance.

## Checklist
- [ ] Identify the common threads across all sources.
- [ ] Note conflicting methodologies or results.
- [ ] Map out the evolution of the technology/concept.
- [ ] Produce a clear "Capability Matrix" or similar visualization.
